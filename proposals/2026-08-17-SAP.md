## Thesis (Stage 2)

> **I own SAP because** it dominates enterprise ERP with near-zero customer switching costs (migration from SAP ERP costs hundreds of millions and takes 3–5 years for large enterprises), and its accelerating S/4HANA cloud migration is converting lumpy one-time license revenue into predictable, high-margin recurring subscription revenue — a structural margin expansion story.

> **I am wrong if:** (1) enterprise IT budgets contract sharply in a recession, delaying cloud migrations; (2) SAP's cloud conversion stalls or faces meaningful competitive displacement by Oracle Fusion or Microsoft Dynamics in core verticals (manufacturing, retail, utilities); or (3) EUR/USD weakens materially, reducing the USD value of euro-denominated earnings for this German ADR.

## Competitive position (Bain lens)
SAP commands ~20–24% global ERP market share, with near-insurmountable switching costs in core industry verticals (automotive OEMs, chemical companies, utilities, global retail). The S/4HANA cloud platform is the centerpiece of its next decade: cloud revenue grew >25% annually as of recent filings, with total cloud backlog converting steadily to recognized revenue. Key risk: the transition from perpetual licenses to subscriptions creates a near-term revenue recognition headwind before the long-term margin benefit fully materializes.
